Where Bristol turns up to walk on a Sunday.

Tessa Greenhow · Director, Clifton office · ★★★★★ 4.9

Clifton sits on the limestone shelf above the Avon Gorge. It's been a postcode you wanted on your driving licence since around 1820 — which is when the crescents went up — and time hasn't really changed that. The shops are the same Whiteladies-Road independents they've always been. The school catchments are the most fought-over in the south west. And the view from the suspension bridge still stops cars at golden hour.

Around 40 % of our Clifton sales last year were to people moving back — Bristolians in their thirties who'd had a London decade and were ready to come home. Most arrived with a clear list of streets. Most ended up on a different street entirely. We can usually tell you the difference between Goldney Road and Goldney Avenue before you've finished saying it.

Living in Clifton

Schools. Clifton College, QEH, Redmaids' High and Clifton High are all within a 12-minute walk of the suspension bridge. State primary catchment is split between Hotwells Primary (good) and Christ Church (outstanding) — the dividing line is Princess Victoria Street, and we know to within ten metres where it lands.

Transport. Park Street to the city centre is a sweaty 9 minutes downhill, 15 minutes back up. Bus 8 / 9 runs every 8 minutes to Bristol Temple Meads. Most Clifton households have one car at most — you don't really need it.

Shops & food. Whiteladies Road is the spine. Clifton Village proper has Toby's, Source, the Albion, and the best deli in Bristol (we'll argue with anyone). Boyces Avenue gets the Saturday brunch crowd.

Green space. Clifton Downs (400 acres) is right there — Bristol's only proper park-park. Sea Walls is the local children's-bike spot. Brandon Hill (Cabot Tower) is a 10-minute walk for sunset.
