The home

A Georgian terrace on Redcliffe's
most-loved Sunday-walking street.

This is the first time number 15 has come to market in 41 years. The current owners — a sculptor and a barrister — bought it shortly after the 1981 floods drained, and have looked after it with the kind of patience that's now almost impossible to buy. The result is a four-bedroom Georgian that feels like a home, not a project.

The house is one of fourteen on the original 1768 parade, set back from the harbour by a private green and a row of pollarded limes. Most of these houses have been chopped into flats. 15 hasn't. You walk in to a single hallway with the original flagstone floor (the owners had the lime-mortar repointed in 2014 by Drew Mahoney's lot — receipts and conservation officer's sign-off in the legal pack), turn right into the front reception, and immediately understand why nobody wants to leave.

The kitchen at the back was rebuilt in 2019 by Plain English — solid English oak, brass tap, Aga in deep teal. It opens to a 38-foot west-facing garden, walled in original red brick, with a workshop at the bottom that's currently a pottery but would make a perfect office or studio. Light pours in from the south side from late morning until sunset.

Upstairs you get four bedrooms across two floors, the master with a recently-fitted ensuite (Lefroy Brooks fittings, Carrara tile). The original sash windows have all been refurbished, weighted, and draught-stripped — they slide silently and you don't hear the city. The wide-plank pine floors are original to the build; they were lifted, cleaned, and re-laid in 2017.

It's listed Grade II. You don't notice — and that, in our view, is the highest possible compliment to the owners.
